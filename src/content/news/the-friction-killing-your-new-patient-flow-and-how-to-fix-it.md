## The 10:45 PM Problem That's Costing You Patients

Picture this scenario. It's nearly 11 PM on a Tuesday. A tired parent finally sits down after wrestling kids through homework, dinner, and the endless bedtime negotiations. Reality check: they're not calling anyone at 10:45 PM. And by tomorrow morning? Life will have already gotten in the way. That patient is gone, likely booking with the practice down the street that offers [online scheduling for dentists](https://mysocialpractice.com/2025/10/online-scheduling-for-dentists/).

This scenario plays out countless times every single day. And here's what makes it even more frustrating: you're losing these patients not because of your clinical skills, your team, or your facility. You're losing them to something much simpler.

Friction.

## What Exactly Is Friction (And Why Should You Care)?

In dental marketing terms, friction is any obstacle, delay, or outdated step that makes scheduling harder than it needs to be. It shows up in sneaky ways you might not even notice: the "call during office hours" message, long intake forms just to request an appointment, the dreaded "we'll call you back to confirm," or worse, getting put on hold while smooth jazz plays endlessly.

Every single one of these moments creates what marketers call a drop-off point, a spot where potential patients simply bail and move on. According to research, [nearly 1 in 5 people abandon the booking process](https://info.mysocialpractice.com/frictionless-future-ebook) if it feels too complicated or takes too long. Most of those people never circle back.

Now consider this: your patients order dinner from their phones in under two minutes. They book haircuts, rideshares, and hotel rooms without ever speaking to a human. When scheduling a teeth cleaning feels more complicated than ordering takeout, your practice starts looking outdated before anyone even walks through your doors.

The solution isn't complicated. It's [eliminating barriers with online scheduling](https://mysocialpractice.com/2025/11/online-dental-scheduler-problems/) that lets patients see real-time availability and book instantly, whether it's 3 PM or 10:45 PM.

## Getting Started Is Easier Than You Think

Adding online scheduling might sound like a big technology project, but most practices go live within a week or two. Here's the typical process:

1. **Connect your practice management software:** The scheduling system integrates with your existing PMS so appointments sync automatically.
2. **Customize your settings:** You choose which services appear online, how long each appointment takes, and which providers are available.
3. **Embed on your website and profiles:** Add the booking widget to your website, Google Business Profile, social media pages, and anywhere else patients might find you.
4. **Go live and promote:** Start booking patients and let them know the new option exists through email, social posts, and in-office signage.

There's no major disruption to your current operations. Your phone lines stay open for patients who prefer calling, while everyone else gets the convenience they're looking for.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does online scheduling typically cost for a dental practice?**

A: Most dental scheduling platforms cost between $100-500 per month depending on features and practice size. However, the ROI typically becomes positive within weeks. If online scheduling captures even 2-3 additional patients monthly, it pays for itself many times over when you factor in lifetime patient value and referrals.

**Q: Will online scheduling create double-bookings or scheduling mistakes?**

A: Not if you choose a system that syncs in real-time with your practice management software. Proper integration means patients only see accurate availability, appointment types match your practice rules, and your schedule updates automatically without manual data entry.

**Q: What happens if a patient books the wrong type of appointment online?**

A: Quality scheduling systems let you customize appointment types with clear descriptions and qualifying questions. You define exactly what's available for online booking versus what requires a phone consultation. Automated confirmation calls can also catch any issues before the patient arrives.

**Q: Can older patients figure out online scheduling?**

A: Most can and will. People of all ages now book flights, hotels, and restaurant reservations online regularly. The key is that online scheduling adds an option without removing phone booking. Patients who prefer calling can still call, while everyone else gets the convenience they want.

**Q: How long does it take to implement online scheduling?**

A: Most dental practices go live within one to two weeks. The actual setup often takes just a few days, with additional [time for staff training and testing](https://www.drlentau.com/blog-articles/ai-is-coming-for-your-dental-practice-but-not-the-way-you-think) before promoting it to patients. It's significantly faster than most practice changes.
